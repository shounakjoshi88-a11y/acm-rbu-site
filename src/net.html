A working picture

Attention, drawn
as it happens.

Two thousand edges, seven layers, and a signal you can send yourself. This is the page's own model of a forward pass, of attention moving between wires, and of the gradient coming back the way it came.

How it works

Click anywhere in the field to send a signal through it.

What you are looking at

Three things happen on those wires every time you fire a pass. Nothing moves through the graph until you do.

01

Forward

Every edge carries an activation from one layer to the next. The bright heads are the ones moving right now; the dim lines are the ones waiting their turn. Seven layers, fourteen nodes in the first and last, forty-four in the middle.

02

Attention

Not every wire matters equally. At any moment a handful of edges take most of the signal, and which handful it is changes from pass to pass. That is the whole trick: the network decides where to look, then looks there harder.

03

Backward

When a pass ends the direction reverses and the field turns warm. Gradients travel the same edges home, and every edge that carried a lot of signal on the way out carries a lot of blame on the way back.

This is a visual model, not a trained network. Every weight on the page is a hash of its own index — no data, no training, nothing to be accurate about. The structure is real; the numbers are decoration.
